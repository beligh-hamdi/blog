If you want a clean example of how markets price the _future_, not the past,
look at the memory-chip giants this week. Samsung and SK Hynix both reported
record Q2 2026 profits. Both stocks fell. The gap between the numbers and the
share price is the actual story — and it tells you something useful about how
the AI trade now works.

## The numbers were genuinely record-breaking

For the April–June 2026 quarter, the two South Korean memory leaders put up
results that would, in any normal cycle, send their stocks soaring:

- **Samsung's semiconductor division** posted operating profit of about 89.2
  trillion won (~$61–62 billion), a more than **250-fold jump** from a year
  earlier, on revenue of 171.5 trillion won — a company record for the third
  quarter running. Net profit rose nearly 1,300% year-on-year to about 71.6
  trillion won, slightly above analyst estimates.
- **SK Hynix** reported record operating profit of about 60.5 trillion won, up
  **557%** year-on-year, on record revenue of about 79.3 trillion won. Its
  operating margin hit 76% — above TSMC's for the third straight quarter.

The driver is the same in both cases: AI-driven demand for high-bandwidth
memory (HBM) and enterprise SSDs has outstripped supply, keeping prices
elevated. Samsung shipped the industry's first HBM4E samples; SK Hynix
started mass shipments of HBM4 and holds roughly 58% of the global HBM market.

## The stocks went the other way

The market's reaction was the opposite of what a "record profits" headline
implies. SK Hynix shares fell about 9.6% on July 29, 2026, on top of a roughly
14.65% drop the prior session. The stock has now lost more than half its value
since its June 2026 record high (it remains up around 115% year-to-date).
Samsung's shares were volatile too, and the broader KOSPI fell about 6% on the
day SK Hynix reported.

So: the biggest memory quarter in history, met with a sell-off.

## Why record earnings became a disappointment

This is the part worth slowing down on, because it's where "the trade" lives.

**Expectations had outrun the results.** SK Hynix's operating profit came in at
60.5 trillion won against an LSEG SmartEstimate nearer 64 trillion; revenue
missed estimates near 84 trillion. When a stock has run up on the assumption
that every quarter will blow the doors off, merely record-breaking is not
enough — the bar moves faster than the earnings. This is the mirror image of
the familiar "bad news priced in." Here the _good_ news was priced in, and
then some.

**Long-term agreements capped the upside.** Roughly half of SK Hynix's sales
run under five-year supply agreements with about ten key customers. Those
LTAs lock in volumes and prices, so the company could not fully pass on the
quarter's rising DRAM prices. Record demand, partially fixed prices — the
very mechanism that makes memory a "supercycle" also blunts how much of it
flows through to a given quarter.

**HBM4 shipments lagged the ramp.** Slower-than-expected HBM4 deliveries
delayed revenue recognition, which mattered precisely because the Street had
timed the ramp into its model.

**The market is now pricing "peak."** Underneath the quarter, the broader
worry is whether hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending keeps compounding.
If capex flattens, today's record profits become the top of the cycle rather
than the floor — and a stock priced for continued compounding gets re-rated
downward even as earnings go up.

## What this means for anyone trading the AI hardware trade

For beligh's readers, the practical reads from this week:

- **Earnings don't move the stock; the surprise versus expectations does.**
  A 557% profit jump can be a sell signal if the Street already discounted
  600%. Track the estimate, not just the print.
- **Supply contracts shape the P&L as much as demand.** When demand is locked
  into multi-year LTAs, a price spike shows up slowly. Don't assume a memory
  shortage translates one-for-one into a single quarter's revenue.
- **"Peak" is a re-rating risk, not an earnings risk.** The bears here aren't
  saying profits will fall — they're saying the growth rate will. A company
  can keep printing records and still see its multiple compress. Position
  sizing has to account for that decoupling.
- **The shortage itself extends the cycle — until it doesn't.** Samsung
  warned that supply shortfalls could worsen into 2027 and 2028 as unmet 2026
  demand rolls forward. That's the bull case, and it's real. The bear case is
  that the market has already priced several more years of it into a stock
  that then missed one quarter's estimate.

## The takeaway

The cleanest lesson from July 30, 2026 isn't "AI memory is booming" — everyone
knows that. It's that _booming_ and _priced for booming_ are different things.
Samsung and SK Hynix just delivered the most profitable quarter the memory
industry has ever seen, and their shareholders lost money on the news. That
happens when expectations travel further than earnings, when contracts delay
the pass-through, and when the market starts asking whether the spending
curve keeps bending up. The fundamentals are record-breaking. The trade is
about what comes after the record.
